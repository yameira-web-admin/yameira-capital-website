A sell limit order always executes at the limit price or higher. With a Trailing Stop Limit, the stop price triggers the order, and then a limit order is placed at your specified price. The examples show the market falling to the stop and then to the limit level before filling at the limit or better—not below it. If the market gaps down past your limit, the order may not fill because you won’t accept worse than your limit.
Hope this helps clarify the difference!

Hi John, thank you for reaching out. The scenario described is not a standard Stop Limit order—it’s a Trailing Stop Limit order, which works differently. In a Trailing Stop Limit:

The stop price triggers the order when the market reaches that level.
The limit price is set based on the trailing amount and adjusts dynamically as the market moves.

For example:

If the stop price is triggered at $113 and the trailing amount sets the limit at $112, the order will aim to sell at $112 if the price continues to decline.
If the price rises by $0.50 and the trailing amount is $1, the new limit becomes $112.50. If the stock then declines, the order will execute at $112.50.

This adjustment mechanism is what differentiates a Trailing Stop Limit from a regular Stop Limit order, which does not change once placed. The purpose is to help capture a better price if the market moves favorably before the stop is triggered. We hope this helps.

Hi Yechiel, thank you for reaching out. A Trailing Stop Limit order is designed to allow an investor to specify a limit on the maximum possible loss without setting a limit on the maximum possible gain. A “Sell” Trailing Stop Limit moves with the market price and continually recalculates the stop trigger price at a fixed amount below the market price based on the user-defined “trailing” amount. The limit order price is also continuously recalculated based on the limit offset. As the market price rises, both the stop price and the limit price will rise by the trail amount and limit offset respectively, but if the stock price falls, the stop price will remain unchanged. When the stop price is hit, a limit order will be submitted at the last calculated limit price. Please review this FAQ for more details on the Order Type: https://www.yameiracapital.com/faq?id=110199999

We hope this helps answer your question!
